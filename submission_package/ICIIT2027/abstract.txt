Shared lockers on university campuses are still opened with mechanical keys or paper codes, cannot be reserved, and give staff no view of forced doors, lost cards or forgotten items; once networked, their unlock signal can also be forged or replayed. This paper presents SmartLocker, a locker rental system in which users reserve a slot on a web map, pay from a prepaid wallet and open it with a personal identification number (PIN), a radio-frequency identification (RFID) card, a fingerprint or a phone page. The ESP32 box takes no access decision: it forwards every credential to the server, which replies UNLOCK or DENY. Abnormal access is detected mainly by a priority-ordered rule engine over device telemetry and server context, which recognises sensor faults, spoofed remote opens, lost cards, forced opening, wrong-user access, items left behind and overdue storage. A machine-learning risk score is kept only as a secondary signal and raises an alert for sessions that no rule explains. On synthetic labelled sessions, the best of four Weka classifiers made almost all of its errors between wrong-user and normal sessions, which share the same telemetry by construction; identity is therefore checked by rule, and the score should be trusted only once real sessions are available. All 83 automated tests and the replayed detection scenarios passed, and the median server processing time of a device request was 8 to 16 ms per endpoint on a local laptop (maximum 38.6 ms). In a pre-deployment simulation of 100 sessions sent through the live device API, a multi-sensor rule prototype reached a macro-F1 of 0.780 against 0.658 for a partial-parity rule comparator, but missed most tamper sessions and stayed below a learned decision tree (0.980); its distributions are assumed, so it does not predict field performance.
